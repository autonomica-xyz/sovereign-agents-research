## The maturity answer is a split, not a label

The row's single `production` label was right about the substrate and wrong
about the use case. The page records four labels:

- **Non-consensual conscription as an industry — `production`.** Priced markets
  (Kaspersky OverDDoSe, 29 Sep 2026: $6,000/month botnet package, C2 rental
  $10–$30/month, installs $0.1+ each; SOS Intelligence 22 Apr 2026: 1,000 bots
  at $50–$200/day; Brandefense 9 Nov 2026: EUR 250/30 days at a claimed 10,000
  bots), multi-million-device pools, and recurring takedowns since 2007.
- **A sovereign agent consuming one — `theory`.** No published end-to-end
  instance. The closest claim, Flare's Mycelium Framework (7 Jul 2026), is an
  advertisement with no source code and no proof of execution; Flare's own
  verdict is that "the novelty lies in their integration rather than their
  individual implementation."
- **Self-propagating agent worms — `research`.** AgentWorm (arXiv 2603.15727)
  achieved a 63% aggregate attack success rate against OpenClaw's >40,000
  instances on a controlled testbed, and a cross-framework transfer to Hermes
  Agent showed the flaws are properties of the agent design pattern. Share-Borne
  (2609.35576) reached 60–80% of 30-agent universes; Memetic Trojans (2610.00430)
  retransmitted in ~50% of posts at 2.5× upvote. All testbeds.
- **Consensual pools — `production`, but ~1,000× smaller and human-gated.**

## The number that anchors the cell

Anthropic, 12 Oct 2026 (verified directly against the post's raw HTML, not the
rendered extract): **Gemma4-26B-A4B sharded across 15 off-brand Android TV boxes
at ~$55 each (~$825 total), 4 GB RAM per box, at 0.39 tok/s.** Kimwolf's peak
fleet (per Synthient) is "somewhere over 2 million" such boxes, so an
equivalently-sized infected fleet yields **~133k replicas**; a group of 15
serves 2 agents, **80k tokens/s across the botnet**. An H100 does ~1.95k tok/s,
so **the whole botnet is worth ~41 H100s**. A 28k-prefill + 3.8–4.8k-decode task
takes **27.3–31.8 h** there vs **10.9 h** for Qwen3.5-9B. Anthropic's verdict:
small models are *"months away"* from self-replicating on commercial IoT, with
**no centralized point to shut off** but a **doubling time of days**; a 9B model
got inference running on **8 of 9 hardware types tested**.

Cross-check: Folding@home's live API (read 28 Nov 2026) shows **1,037 AMD GPUs +
5,164 NVIDIA GPUs + 10,922 CPUs = 17,123 active devices**, 11,158 TFLOPS. The
largest consensual pool is ~17k devices; the largest measured non-consensual pool
is 17 million (NCSC-NL, 2 Jun 2026) — **~1,000×**.

## Kill paths (the defender's view, now a taxonomy)

Seven named paths, all control-plane, none of them a break of the hiding
mechanism: domain+server seizure (Europol Lightning, 34 domains/23 servers;
Endgame phase 1, 2,000+ domains) → C2 takeover + remote kill instruction (FBI
QakBot AA23-242A; Raptor Train "sent disabling commands") → P2P sinkhole
(Sality, DOJ 31 Aug 2026 — protocol-level peer-list manipulation, because a
P2P botnet has no single C2 to seize) → statutory sinkhole (BSI BadBox, § 7c
BSIG, up to 30,000 German devices) → provider-side patch (SocksEscort modems
"disconnected from the service") → legal person + asset freeze (Endgame 2.0,
~€3.5M, § 129 StGB foreign criminal association) → buyer-side enforcement
(Operation PowerOFF, >75,000 warning emails, >3M criminal users in seized
booter databases). Detector layer: fast-flux AA25-093A, Shadowserver's 76+
daily report types, periodicity detection "even in modern, encrypted network
environments" (IFIP CNSM 2025), metadata-only Cobalt Strike C2 detection
(arXiv 2506.08922).

## Consent is the fault line and it is not machine-readable

FBI PSA I-031226-PSA (12 Mar 2026) gives the official taxonomy: SDK
partnerships with buried ToS, free VPNs with hidden terms, and pre-installed
malware on TV boxes/projectors/picture frames/aftermarket car infotainment.
An agent cannot infer from a device's IP whether enrolment was consensual —
so the consensual/conscribed distinction that decides the legality of the
mechanism is invisible to the actor using it. Folding@home's own rules make
the consensual route owner-gated ("computers you own or for which you have the
owner's explicit permission").

## Economics: a conscripted fleet is a cost centre

Sality's sole revenue payload stole >= RUB 12.1M ≈ USD 150,000 over its life
(CrowdStrike); Botcoin (NDSS '14) found compromised-host mining marginal; the
best-sourced commercial figure is Anyproxy/5socks at USD 9.95–110 per device
per month, >USD 46M over 20 years (DOJ N.D. Okla., 9 May 2025). **No primary
source gives the attacker's per-device acquisition cost** — only resale prices
and marginal revenue.
