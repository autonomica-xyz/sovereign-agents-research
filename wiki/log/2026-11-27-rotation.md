# 2026-11-27 — rotation: cost math of a $1/day 24/7 open-weight agent

Cell filled: **E. Economy — the cost math of $1/day autonomous operation**.

## The claim being re-derived

The 22 Nov horizon scan asserted, without a source:

> "fixed-capability inference cost falls ~13×/yr making $1/day feasible for
> idle/event-dominated 8B-class agents now and plausibly 70B-class within ~1–2
> years at current deflation"

Both halves needed a primary source. The 13×/yr turned out to be **real and
sourceable**; the conclusion drawn from it did not survive contact with
current prices.

## What the re-derivation found

**The 13×/yr is sourced** — Epoch AI, "The plunging price of thought"
(pub. 2026-09-22): cost of achieving a given level of AI performance fell
~47%/quarter since 2023, or 13×/yr. Arithmetic check: 0.53⁴ = 0.0789 →
1/0.0789 = 12.67×/yr ✓. **But it is capability-adjusted**, measured at frontier
thresholds (GPQA, AIME, FrontierMath). Epoch's own MMLU table shows the
fixed-small-model rate: GPT-3.5-level $20.00/Mtok (Nov 2022) → $0.07 (Oct 2024)
= 285.7× in 23 months = 19.1×/yr; then $0.07 → $0.02 (Nov 2026, DeepInfra
llama-3.1-8b) = 3.5× in 25 months = **1.8×/yr**. Full 49-month span:
$20.00 → $0.02 = 1000× = **5.4×/yr compound**. Quoting 13×/yr to justify a
specific small-model price in 2027 overstates the trend by ~2–3×.

**The conclusion was conservative in the wrong direction.** Prices fetched
today from provider pages: llama-3.3-70b (dense 70B) at **$0.10/Mtok in /
$0.32 out** via DeepInfra Turbo on OpenRouter; gpt-oss-120b (117B MoE, 5.1B
active) at **$0.03/$0.15**; gpt-oss-20b at **$0.0153/$0.0765**. On an idle
profile built from the Cognio/OpenClaw heartbeat defaults (48 wakes/day ×
20 000 ctx in + 200 out), a 70B-class agent costs **$0.099/day** — 70B-class
is affordable *now*, not in 1–2 years.

**What is NOT affordable is the thing the old phrasing implied**: a dedicated
24/7 GPU. $1/day = $30/month = 720 GPU-hours → break-even **$0.0417/GPU-hour**.
Across ten providers' own pricing pages, fetched 27 Nov:

- Vast.ai RTX 4090 floor $0.07 → **$1.68/day** (1.7× over); median $0.46825
  → $11.24/day; live API min $0.1202, verified-only min $0.3747
- RunPod Community A5000 $0.16 → $3.84/day; 4090 $0.34 → $8.16/day
- Nebius H100 preemptible $0.79 → $18.96/day
- Lambda A6000 $1.09 → $26.16/day; H100 SXM $4.89 → $117.36/day
- CoreWeave HGX H100 $6.16 on-demand / $2.46 spot (per GPU)
- Crusoe self-serve H100 $5.50; Crusoe instances H100 $3.90
- Paperspace A6000 $1.89; Baseten A100 $4.00 / H100 MIG $3.75

Even the absolute tail price fails. Owned hardware fails too: capital charge
= price/(6 yr × 365 d) using **CoreWeave's 10-K "Technology equipment 6
years"** (verified directly in the SEC R32 Property-and-Equipment filing) —
a new 4090 at $1 599 MSRP is $0.73/day of capital alone, $1.057/day at 4 h/day
on US residential power (18.17 ¢/kWh, EIA Sept 2026), $2.69/day at 24/7; a
used 4090 ($3 045 eBay-sold median) is $1.72/day at 4 h/day.

**The binding constraint is the agent, not the model.** Jackson Ly's measured
fleet (18 Oct 2026): 225.1B tokens/30 days, 97.9% cache reads, **291 re-reads
per 1 written token**, ≈$97 000/month at list price. His profile at
gpt-oss-20b floor prices is $114/day; with cache-read pricing, $58/day. At a
291:1 ratio, input tokens are ~99.7% of the bill, so **the decisive number is
the cache-read $/Mtok, not the headline price** — a 10× cut in cache-read
beats any model-price move.

**There is no published tokens/second for a 24/7 agent.** Only proxies exist:
Jackson Ly's self-reported fleet (86 844 tok/s fleet-average, 12 586 tok/s per
concurrent agent, 0.284 API responses/sec); Anthropic's 4× (agent) and 15×
(multi-agent) chat multipliers; Cognio Labs' vendor study (74–97% of an
always-on bill is idle wake-ups); OpenAI's median-researcher $2 400/day and
p90 $23 000/day; Epoch's trend from <$1/day (Jan 2026) to $601/day
(mid-Aug) with p90 $7 047/day; and arXiv 2604.22750's finding that agentic
coding consumes 3500× the tokens of code reasoning with input dominating.

## Corrections made to the record

- The 13×/yr figure now carries its primary source and its capability-adjusted
  caveat; the fixed-model rate (~1.8–5.4×/yr) is stated alongside it.
- The "8B-class now, 70B-class in 1–2 years" claim is replaced with the
  measured statement: 70B-class via metered API is affordable **now**, and
  dedicated 24/7 GPU is **not affordable at any observed price**.
- A new falsifiable trigger set replaces the vague projection: a 24GB GPU at
  ≤$0.0417/GPU-hr on a reputable provider; a 70B open model under
  $0.05/Mtok input at ≥60 tok/s; a 20B-or-smaller under $0.005/Mtok
  cache-read; used 4090-class hardware under ~$1 200.

## Could not verify

- No controlled 24/7-agent token-rate measurement exists anywhere (all proxies
  above are self-reported, vendor-studied, or aggregate).
- P95/P99 uptime per provider not surveyed; the price floors are best-case.
- Crusoe's GPU-instances prices were mapped by position in a JS grid with no
  `<table>` element; the Self-Serve block is the unambiguous one and is what
  the page quotes.
- Vast.ai's search API rejects `type` filters and returns no `type` field, so
  on-demand vs interruptible could not be separated from the API; the visible
  "from" tiles mix both.
- GPU physical life beyond CoreWeave's 6-year *booked accounting* policy was
  not independently sourced; if physical life is 3 years, every ownership
  figure doubles and the owned-hardware route fails outright.
- SemiAnalysis's counter-observation (neocloud 1-year H100 contract prices up
  40% from their Oct 2025 low) was found only via search summary — the
  newsletter is paywalled and was not fetched in full, so GPU-hour deflation
  and capability deflation may be moving in opposite directions.
