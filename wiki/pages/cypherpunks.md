# Cypherpunks

> The mailing-list movement (from 1992) that wrote the toolkit an organism
> uses: Tim May's "Crypto Anarchist Manifesto" (1988) predicted anonymous
> markets that states could not stop; Eric Hughes' "A Cypherpunk's Manifesto"
> (1993) said "Cypherpunks write code". Remailers, ecash, PGP, b-money and bit
> gold came out of this culture.

## Details

- May: cryptography will "alter completely the nature of government
  regulation, the ability to tax and control economic interactions".
- Hughes: "Privacy is necessary for an open society in the electronic age";
  privacy as selective self-revelation, which is the
  [privacy-organ](privacy-organ.md) idea from the organism's side.
- Products of the milieu: [digicash](digicash.md) (Chaum), anonymous
  remailers (mixnets' ancestors), [b-money](b-money.md) (Wei Dai, 1998),
  [szabo-smart-contracts](szabo-smart-contracts.md) and bit gold, Hashcash
  (Adam Back, 1997), then [bitcoin](bitcoin.md) (2008).

## How it serves the organism

Its whole stack (anonymous money, encrypted channels, pseudonymous identity)
was designed for actors that states want to read or stop.

## How it fails

Designed for human pseudonyms; assumed a human behind every key.
