# Szabo: smart contracts and bit gold

> Nick Szabo defined a smart contract (1994) as "a computerized transaction
> protocol that executes the terms of a contract", and in "Formalizing and
> Securing Relationships on Public Networks" (First Monday, 1997) described
> contracts embedded in hardware and software so breach is expensive. His bit
> gold (described 2005, designed earlier) chained proof-of-work into scarce
> digital property.

## Details

- The vending machine is his canonical example: a contract enforced by the
  machine, not by courts. An organism lives entirely on such contracts
  because it has no court to go to.
- Smart property: control of physical property (cars, locks) by keys, which
  the 2013 agent design reused for SSH keys sold as property
  ([bitcoin-wiki-agents](bitcoin-wiki-agents.md)).
- Bit gold prefigured Bitcoin's design.

## How it serves the organism

The theory of self-enforcing agreements, which is the only kind an entity
without legal standing can rely on ([legal-personhood](legal-personhood.md)).

## How it fails

Smart contracts enforce code, not intent; bugs and oracles are the attack
surface ([the-dao](the-dao.md)).
