# DACs and "DAOs, DACs, DAs"

> In 2013 Dan Larimer and Vitalik Buterin wrote about Decentralized
> Autonomous Corporations: software entities that hold capital and pay
> participants. Buterin's "DAOs, DACs, DAs and More: An Incomplete Terminology
> Guide" (Ethereum blog, 6 May 2014) separated them by where the automation
> sits, and defined the **decentralized autonomous agent (DA)** as the extreme
> case: no human involvement at all, which is the organism.

## Details

- Buterin's grid: "automation at the center, humans at the edges" (DAO/DAC)
  versus "humans at the center" (ordinary organisations). A DA has "no
  humans involved at all"; his examples include a self-replicating cloud
  computing service that rents servers and spawns copies, an echo of the
  Bitcoin Wiki design ([bitcoin-wiki-agents](bitcoin-wiki-agents.md)).
- Larimer framed Bitcoin itself as a DAC whose shareholders are coin
  holders; that view led to BitShares and later EOS.
- Buterin's "Bootstrapping a Decentralized Autonomous Corporation"
  (Bitcoin Magazine, Sep 2013) discussed what such entities need: capital,
  ways to pay humans, and code-based internal rules.

## How it serves the organism

Gave the organism its first precise vocabulary: an entity whose decisions
come from code and whose capital is its own.

## How it fails

The DAO wave that followed put humans back at the centre (token votes), and
the first big one was drained ([the-dao](the-dao.md)).
