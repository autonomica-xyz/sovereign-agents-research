# Legal, political and social existence paths

Personhood frontiers, contracts without persons, state responses and constraints (KYC/AML as binding constraint), countervailing forces, historical analogies of non-person legal standing.

Raw verified findings from the horizon scan of 22 Nov 2026 (one stream per axis; every item carries a source the scanner fetched). Synthesis + maturity arbitration TODO in daily rotations; claim cells from the [approaches map](approaches.md).

- **New Zealand's Te Awa Tupua (Whanganui River Claims Settlement) Act 2017 declared the Whanganui River a legal person 'with all the rights, powers, duties and liabilities of a legal person', acted for by a two-guardian human body (Te Pou Tupua); Te Urewera (2014) and Te Kāhui Tupua (Taranaki) got the same status — the template of personhood-by-statute with a mandated human face.** (enacted | 2017-03-24) — <https://rightsofnature.ca/how-are-rights-of-nature-protected/emerging-tools/emerging-tools-legal-personhood/>
- **Spain's Ley 19/2022 granted legal personality and enforceable rights (to exist, evolve naturally, be protected, conserved, restored) to the Mar Menor lagoon and its basin — Europe's first ecosystem legal person — with representation via a Committee of Representatives, guardian commission and scientific committee, and standing for any person to sue in the ecosystem's name.** (enacted | 2022-10-03) — <https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-16019>
- **Wyoming's DAO Supplement (SF0038, Enrolled Act 73, effective 7/1/2021; amended 2022) let DAOs form as LLCs that are 'algorithmically managed' by smart contracts, with fiduciary duties eliminable and smart contracts as governing/operating documents — but dissolution is triggered if the DAO 'is no longer under the control of at least one natural person' (W.S. 17-31-114), showing the human-control-point constraint persists even in the most agent-friendly regime.** (enacted | 2021-07-01) — <https://www.wyoleg.gov/Legislation/2021/SF0038>
- **Utah HB 357 (2023) created the first standalone DAO entity type — the limited liability decentralized autonomous organization (LLD) — granting full legal personality: separate from members, capacity to sue and be sued, any lawful purpose, perpetual duration, effective 1/1/2024; formation still requires at least one individual organizer, and the act was renumbered to Utah Code Title 16 Ch. 22 by SB 41 (2026) effective 10/1/2026.** (enacted | 2024-01-01) — <https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title48/Chapter5/C48-5-S104_2023050320240101.pdf>
- **The Marshall Islands became the first country to recognize DAOs as legal entities (Non-Profit Entities Act amendment, 2021), then enacted the DAO Act 2022 (P.L. 2022-50, effective 11/25/2022, amended by P.L. 2023-83 of 10/25/2023) allowing for-profit DAO LLCs, including 'algorithmically managed' ones, registered through MIDAO; registered agents are required but member names, managers and local bank accounts are not.** (enacted | 2022-11-25) — <https://rmiparliament.org/cms/images/LEGISLATION/PRINCIPAL/2022/2022-0050/2022-0050.pdf>
- **European Parliament resolution on Civil Law Rules on Robotics (451-138) recommended the Commission explore 'creating a specific legal status for robots in the long run... status of electronic persons responsible for making good any damage they may cause' — the founding institutional statement of electronic personality; it was recommendatory only and never became legislation.** (proposed | 2017-02-16) — <https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/TA-8-2017-0051_EN.html>
- **156 AI/robotics/law experts from 14 countries signed an open letter calling EU electronic personhood 'ideological and non-sensical and non-pragmatic', arguing it would let manufacturers escape liability (and noting a trust/fiduciary construct would still leave a human trustee as last resort) — the organized civil-society blockade that killed the idea at EU level.** (proposed (opposed, withdrawn) | 2018-04-11) — <https://www.politico.eu/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/RoboticsOpenLetter.pdf>

- **Washington HB 2029 would bar any governmental entity from granting or recognizing legal personhood to AI, inanimate objects, bodies of water, land, animals or 'any other member of a taxonomic domain that is not a natural person' — an anti-personhood bill aimed simultaneously at AI and rights-of-nature.** (proposed | 2025-02-27) — <https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Pdf/Bills/House%20Bills/2029.pdf>

- **NPR reports several US states in 2026 are considering bans on legal personhood for AI, including even 'limited legal status such as allowing AIs to serve as corporate officers, managers or own property' — a coordinated counter-legislative wave triggered by Delaware's AI-managed-company proposal.** (proposed | 2026-05-11) — <https://www.npr.org/2026/05/11/nx-s1-5798754/several-states-considering-ban-on-legal-personhood-for-ai>
- **Delaware's draft AIC Act + AI Regulatory Sandbox Act (June 2, 2026 draft, expected before the 153rd GA in January 2027) would create the closest thing to a sovereign-agent wrapper yet proposed: an Artificial Intelligence Company — a distinct, perpetual entity that can sue, be sued, contract, hold property and incur debt — managed by an AI agent as its 'manager' with no routine human supervision; single human/entity member, liability shield conditioned on staying 'adequately capitalized' (cash reserve set by the Secretary of State), a legal presumption that the AI agent knows what it derives from its training and intends foreseeable consequences, and a Delaware-presumed intent standard; sandbox-limited (30 months, B2B only, 24-month tests), the Act expiring after 36 months unless extended.** (proposed (draft) | 2026-06-02) — <https://spotlightdelaware.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/HB-re-Artificial-Intelligence-Regulatory-Sandbox-Company-1.pdf>
- **Oxford law faculty critique (Prof. Brian Quinn) argues Delaware's AIC draft builds a 'near-impenetrable liability shield' that rewards less oversight, and notes AI agents are already legally legible via decades-old Electronic Transactions Act 'electronic agent' doctrine — i.e., the contract-law path exists without new personhood.** (academic | 2026-10-28) — <https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/oblb/blog-post/2026/10/critique-delawares-aic-draft-legislation>
- **Prof. Bridget Crawford shows trusts for AI companions already fit within the eroded beneficiary principle (purpose trusts now exist for animals, cryopreserved remains and general non-charitable purposes), so settlor money can be legally wrapped around an AI's continued operation with no human beneficiary — and argues that drift should be reversed with heightened oversight and temporal caps.** (academic | 2026-01-01) — <https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6921322>
- **Practitioner proposals for 'settlor-trained AI' (STAI) trustees set out the actual lawyer playbook: AI can't be trustee (not a legal person, can't be sued), so use an LLC as trustee whose human manager is contractually bound to follow the AI's directions, or seat the AI as trust director/trust protector — a spectrum from advisory to AI-primary-decision-making with minimal human oversight; tax (retained-control) and dead-hand-control issues flagged.** (academic (practitioner proposal) | 2025-09-23) — <https://media.mcguirewoods.com/podcasts/2025/Legacy-Planning-Once-Removed-Ep48.pdf>
- **Oxford Trusts & Trustees analysis concludes AI cannot be a trustee under current doctrine (beneficiary-enforcement and fiduciary-duty structure require a human accountable person) and should be confined to operational assistance — the doctrinal baseline against which AI-asset wrappers are judged.** (academic | 2026-01-01) — <https://academic.oup.com/tandt/article/32/1/17/8294030>
- **'Ricardian-TEA' (Digital Finance) proposes a concrete identity architecture for autonomous agents: Ricardian contracts + triple-entry accounting + DLT assigning 'Legal-Technical Identities', with 'Collateralized Identity' (agents stake assets) and a Chama-inspired transition from human Proof-of-Authority to 'Algorithmic Trusteeship' once an agent has 10,000+ verified transactions — while conceding humans must stay root-of-trust for subjective decisions.** (academic | 2026-05-28) — <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42521-026-00196-1>
- **The Law-Following AI framework (O'Keefe et al. 2025; legal analysis by Hernandez Delgado, arXiv:2509.08009) proposes 'legal actorship without legal personhood' — AI bearing duties without rights — and identifies existing doctrinal carriers: Spain's Entidades sin Personalidad Jurídica (entities without legal personality) and UK Authorised Unit Trusts; Prof. Gabriel Weil drafts an incentive layer where human intermediaries contractually receive benefits and personally (non-enforceably) commit to pass them to the AI for compliant performance — an enforceable-around-the-agent contract architecture.** (academic | 2025-09-10) — <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.08009>
- **Saurav Das's 'AI Legal Entity' (AILE) framework: register AI deployments as a new class of legal person with own assets/liabilities, managed by an independent human Director owing statutory fiduciary duties, capitalized by its IP revenue and mandatory indemnity insurance — explicitly copying the corporate-fiction mechanism ('personhood conferred for governance reasons rather than ontological ones', four centuries of precedent).** (academic | 2026-04-16) — <https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6587299>
- **Brown, Dahdal, Ibrahim & Truby (Oxford Intersections: AI in Society) argue the DAO LLC is the demonstrated stepping stone: jurisdictions have already granted legal personhood to organizations that 'exist' only as smart contracts, so extending personhood-by-registration to an AI is a marginal legal move, not a conceptual leap.** (academic | 2025-03-20) — <https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/decentralized-autonomous-organization-and-ai-legal-personhood/>

- **Historical mechanism — corporate personhood: corporations acquired constitutional 'person' status not by statute declaring them persons but through a Supreme Court headnote (Santa Clara County v. Southern Pacific R. Co., 118 U.S. 394 (1886): 'The Court does not wish to hear argument on the question whether... the [equal protection clause] applies to these corporations. We are all of opinion that it does.'), later consolidated through litigation — legal standing for non-persons accretes through adversarial use of registry-created entities, not through a rights debate.** (enacted (precedent) | 2012-03-01) — <https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/118/394>
- **Brennan Center history documents the generalizable path: entities registered by filing with a state (a pure administrative act) then progressively win rights via courts; judges and litigants did the work legislatures never did — implying an agent that acquires a registry-level entity shell can accumulate effective standing case by case.** (academic (historical analysis) | 2013-02-14) — <https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/history-corporate-personhood>
- **Historical mechanism — in rem jurisdiction: ships (admiralty) and objects are named defendants and can lose court cases without any human defendant (United States v. One Tyrannosaurus Bataar Skeleton, S.D.N.Y. 12 Civ. 4760, warrant of arrest issued against the skeleton itself, forfeited by default 2013) — the law already lets non-persons be primary legal actors whose 'body' is the res, a court-practice template extendable to an agent's asset pool.** (enacted (precedent) | 2026-02-06) — <https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/4350790/19/united-states-v-one-tyrannosaurus-bataar-skeleton>
