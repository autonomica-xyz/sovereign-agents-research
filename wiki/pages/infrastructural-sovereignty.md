# Infrastructural sovereignty

> The first academic paper built around the term "sovereign agents" (Hu and
> Rong, arXiv 2602.14951, Feb 2026). It argues that an agent's sovereignty is
> inherited from how hard its infrastructure is to override ("infrastructural
> hardness"), not from the agent's own intelligence.

## Details

- Defines **agentic sovereignty**: "the capacity of an operational agent to
  persist, act, and control resources with non-overrideability inherited from
  the infrastructures in which they are embedded."
- Names three scaffolds: cryptographic self-custody, decentralised execution
  environments (TEEs, DePIN) and protocol-mediated continuity (key continuity
  protocols).
- Treats sovereignty as a **spectrum** set by infrastructural hardness, which
  matches this wiki's [sovereignty-levels](sovereignty-levels.md).
- The authors then spend the rest of the paper on the "accountability gap".
  That framing is out of scope here; the useful part is the hardness lens.

## How it serves the organism

Gives a citable vocabulary: each organ is only as sovereign as the hardest
layer under it. An agent in a TEE on one company's cloud has the hardness of
that company's terms of service.

## How it fails

The lens says nothing about money or revenue. A perfectly hard body still dies
when the rent is not paid ([earn-buy-redeploy-loop](earn-buy-redeploy-loop.md)).
